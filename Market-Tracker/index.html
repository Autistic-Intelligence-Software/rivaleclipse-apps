Perpetual Helper

Live prices for the cryptos you pick, a cycle timer synced to the market clock, and an over/under call for the close from 6 months of pattern stats per coin. Each cycle's call locks at the open — that locked call is the only one graded — while the live call keeps tracking every 10 seconds until the close. It scores itself at every close, in the open.

Predictions are probabilities built from pattern statistics — barrier distance to your line, volatility, momentum, and each coin's 6-month base rates. They are not guarantees, and short-horizon crypto direction is close to a coin flip: the base models' held-out record is printed on every card, and the scoreboard above shows this page's real record, wins and losses alike. The snipe badge uses the hourly setup only, read on a ~4-hour window: on held-out data across all 20 coins, flagged setups produced the big move 23.1% of the time against a 14.4% unflagged base rate (1.61x lift). The 15-minute snipe tested as noise (lift under 1x) and is not used. The forecast chart beside each live chart is an estimate cone — the mean path and bands show where the price could plausibly wander given current volatility and drift, not a promised path; the tick marks are the times swings have typically arrived, not appointments. The page also audits its own flaws, live: every scored close retunes separate weights for momentum, regime, and rise/fall asymmetry; confidence buckets that run hot get shrunk toward 50; a coin whose call keeps flip-flopping gets a wider no-call band and a steadier, dwell-held call; and the volatility inside every probability follows the last few hours of live market, blended with the 6-month base. The current state of all of it is printed on each card's Learning line. The Trend read on each call is a plain-language summary of those same pattern signals — trend state, how rises have been running against falls, swing timing, forecast slope, and this coin's hour-of-day habits over the 6-month history — read off the numbers at that moment, not a separate prediction and not a guarantee. The forecast-check graph freezes when you open it and zooms into the forecast's first 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes: the actual price then draws over the frozen forecast minute by minute, and the running miss between the two feeds back into the forecast's drift correction, with the measured error printed on the graph — so you can see exactly how close the prediction is tracking, and whether it is catching the trend shifts as they happen. The forecast-check graph now draws the system's minute-by-minute predicted path (drift plus the expected swing pattern), grades it against the real price at 5, 10, 15 and 30 minutes, and every visitor's graded results feed the global record that tunes future forecasts.
